## What's missing

The always-on guidance says how to *fill* an interactive decision card — never batch several decisions into one, never let its terseness drop the pros and cons or the argued lean, keep its question to one short line with the detail in its options — and says nothing about when to open one rather than writing prose.

The rule that answers it exists, but only in a maintainer's personal global guidance, which no consumer receives: prose by default, and a card only for a genuine side-by-side of concrete options or a trivial either/or. Its reasoning is the one the shipped bullet already half-states — a card's format strips the pros and cons, the risks, and the argued lean, which is exactly what the human decides on.

## Why it matters

This is the fork the consumer-operating-versus-repo-development split exists to prevent: an operating rule that binds every session lives in a file that does not materialize into a consumer repo. A consumer's assistant therefore reads a complete account of how a card must be filled and no account of whether to reach for one, so the container choice is made by taste, per turn.

The failure is quiet in the same way the register defect was. A card offered where prose was wanted still looks compliant — it carries options and a lean — and the loss is visible only to the human who wanted the argument and got a widget instead.

## Resolution shape

One clause in the same bullet, which is already the container-versus-content paragraph: prose by default; a card only for a trivial either/or or a genuine side-by-side comparison.

The per-turn budget is at its ceiling, so this lands with the same call the register clause took — raise the ceiling to fit a rule that earns its place rather than compress the rule to fit the ceiling — or by tightening existing prose in that bullet. Two or three lines is the right size.

The assertion reuses the bullet extractor the one-decision tests already share, rather than adding a second one.

Worth settling while editing: whether this is the same rule as the register clause — one bullet, one concern, how a decision is offered — or a separate rule about container choice that earns its own bullet. That bullet is already the longest in the fragment.
